Inferential Hypothesis Testing

Evaluate whether observed atmospheric differences represent statistically significant phenomena or random sampling variance.

Parametric Test 1

Two-Sample Independent Welch's t-test

PM2.5 Concentrations: Winter vs. Summer Seasonal Inversion

H₀ (Null): μ_winter = μ_summer (Equal mean PM2.5 levels)
H₁ (Alt): μ_winter > μ_summer (Winter pollution is significantly higher)
Significance Level α = 0.05
ML Implication: High statistical difference justifies including Season as an encoded categorical feature or interaction term.
Parametric Test 2

One-Way ANOVA (Analysis of Variance)

AQI Variation across All Four Seasons (Winter, Spring, Summer, Autumn)

H₀: μ₁ = μ₂ = μ₃ = μ₄ (All seasonal mean AQIs are equivalent)
H₁: At least one season differs significantly in mean AQI
Metric: F-Ratio = (Between-Group Var) / (Within-Group Var)
ML Implication: High F-statistic confirms high between-group variance; seasonal dummy variables provide genuine signal without overfitting.
Non-Parametric Test

Chi-Square (χ²) Test of Independence

Contingency: Wind Speed Tier (<12 km/h vs >12 km/h) vs Hazardous AQI Status

H₀: Wind speed tier and Hazard status are independent
H₁: Significant association between calm wind conditions and hazard events
ML Implication: Significant categorical dependency proves non-linear threshold interaction; crucial for decision trees and random forests.
